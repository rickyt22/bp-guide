How to Use Your Blood Pressure Monitor

Simple step-by-step instructions — your care team is always here to help.

Welcome to 22RPM! Your doctor has set you up with a blood pressure monitor so your care team can keep an eye on your health from home. This guide will walk you through everything — one easy step at a time. If you get stuck at any point, email us and we will walk you through it.
Getting started
1

Download the 22RPM App

Free to download on iPhone and Android

Review: this step is unchanged from the live page, but it says the app is on Google Play. Nobody can confirm that from the repo, and the Android build on main crashes at launch. Decide before publishing: keep, or say "iPhone now, Android coming soon."

The 22RPM app connects your blood pressure monitor to your care team. You only need to download it once.

  1. Pick up your phone and find the App Store (iPhone) or Google Play Store (Android).
  2. Tap the search bar and type: 22RPM
  3. Tap Get (iPhone) or Install (Android) and wait for it to finish.
  4. Once downloaded, tap Open to launch the app.
💡

Need help? Ask a family member, or email us at info@twentytwohealth.com and we will help you.

2

Log In to the App

Use the login and password your care team gave you

  1. Open the 22RPM app. You will see the login screen.
  2. In the box called Email or Username, type the email, username, or phone number your care team gave you.
  3. Type your password in the box below it.
  4. Tap the big Login button.
  5. A box called OTP Verification will ask for a 6-digit code. We send it by email, or by text message if you logged in with your phone number. The box always says “email,” so check your text messages too. Type the code and tap Verify.
📱

iPhone: the first time you open the app, before you log in, your iPhone asks if 22 RPM can use Bluetooth. Tap Allow (some iPhones say OK). The app needs Bluetooth to talk to your monitor.

👆

Log in faster next time. After you log in, the app may ask to turn on quick login. Tap Enable. Next time you will not need your password or the 6-digit code.

iPhone: the message says “Enable Face ID?”. It says Face ID even on iPhones with a Home button. Those phones use your fingerprint instead. If it does not work, tap Use Password Instead.

Android: STUB. The Android wording is not final (see checklist B3).

Connecting your device

These steps are different on iPhone and Android. Follow the part for your phone, then go to Step 5.

iPhone

Using an iPhone? Follow Steps 3 and 4 below.

3

Open Blood Pressure

One tap from your home screen

  1. After you log in, you will see your home screen. It says Hello and your name at the top.
  2. Tap the Blood Pressure card, under “Today's Readings.” You can also tap the reminder above it that says “Time for your blood pressure reading today.”
4

Connect Your Monitor

You only do this once

  1. Make sure Bluetooth is turned on on your phone. Go to Settings → Bluetooth, and check the switch is green.
  2. Press and hold the power button on your monitor until it turns on.
  3. On the Blood Pressure screen, tap the Connect button at the top.
    If the button is gray and says Connecting..., just wait. A list will open by itself in about 15 seconds.
  4. A list called Connect to BP Device opens. Tap your monitor. Its name starts with BP2, like BP2A.
  5. The list closes by itself. A green bar says “Connected. Press Start on the BP machine to take your reading.” You are ready.
💬

The first time, you may see “Reconnecting to your device…” or “Couldn't connect to the cuff.” This is normal. Wait for the list, then tap your monitor.

✅

Next time is easier. Turn on your monitor, then open Blood Pressure. The app finds your monitor by itself. You will see “Reconnecting to your device…” and then the green bar. You do not need to tap Connect again.

🔊

The app talks. You may hear “Device connected” or “Measurement started.” That is normal.

📶

Keep your phone close by — within arm's reach during the reading.

Taking your reading
5

Put On the Arm Cuff

Correct placement gives you an accurate reading

Before putting on the cuff, sit quietly for 5 minutes. Do not drink coffee, smoke, or exercise for 30 minutes before your reading.

Use your left arm Palm facing up, resting on a table
2 finger-widths above the elbow Bottom edge just above the inner elbow crease
On bare skin Roll your sleeve up first
Arm at heart height Arm flat on the table, at chest level
⚠️

During your reading: sit still, breathe normally, and do not talk.

6

Take Your Reading and Check the Result

Your reading goes straight to your care team

  1. Press the Start button on your monitor. The cuff will inflate on its own — this is normal.
  2. Sit still and breathe normally until the cuff deflates, about 30–60 seconds.
  3. Your reading appears in the app automatically — blood pressure numbers and pulse rate.

iPhone: a box pops up that says Reading sent and “Shared with your care team.” Tap Done.

If the box says Reading saved instead, your phone is not online right now. Your reading is safe. It will send by itself when your phone is back on Wi-Fi or cell service. You do not need to take it again.

When your monitor turns itself off after the reading, the app may say “Device disconnected” out loud. Then it may show “Couldn't connect to the cuff” and the device list. That is normal. Your reading is already saved. Tap Close.

Android: STUB. Android shows a short message instead of a "Reading sent" box, and until the outbox lands it does not save readings taken offline (checklist B7).

🎉

That's it. Your reading is done and your care team has received it. You can close the app.

📅

When to take readings: once in the morning before your medications, and once in the evening. Follow your doctor's schedule.

Understanding your numbers
📊

What Do Your Numbers Mean?

Your care team watches these — but here is a simple guide

Blood pressure is two numbers, like 120/78. The top number is systolic (when the heart beats). The bottom number is diastolic (between beats).

Blood pressure categories
Category Top number Bottom number What it means
Low Below 90 or below 60 This reading is lower than usual. For some people that is normal and causes no problems. Tell your care team if you feel dizzy, lightheaded, faint, weak, sick to your stomach or confused, or if your vision blurs or your heart pounds or flutters.
Normal Below 120 and below 80 Great — keep it up.
Elevated 120–129 and below 80 A little high — your team will monitor it.
High Stage 1 130–139 or 80–89 Your team may contact you.
High Stage 2 140 or higher or 90 or higher Contact your team soon.
Crisis ⚠️ Over 180 or over 120 Call 911 right away.

Category boundaries follow the American Heart Association.

🚨

Call 911 immediately if your reading is over 180/120 and you have symptoms: severe headache, chest pain, trouble breathing, or changes in your vision.

Common questions

I forgot my username or password. What do I do?

Email your 22RPM care coordinator at info@twentytwohealth.com and they will reset it for you quickly.

My device won't connect. What should I do?

iPhone: Make sure your monitor is on. Turn Bluetooth off and back on. Go back to the home screen, then open Blood Pressure again. Wait for the list to open, and tap your monitor. If it still will not connect, email us.

STUB, Android answer: pending (Location switch, permissions, scan every visit; see checklist).

Do I need Wi-Fi for my readings to send?

Your phone needs Wi-Fi or cell service to send a reading. On iPhone, if you take a reading without internet, the app says Reading saved and sends it by itself once you are back online.

Android: today a reading taken offline is lost, so this promise is iPhone-only until the Android outbox ships (checklist B7).

My reading seems very high or low. Should I worry?

Wait 2–3 minutes and take it again. If it is still over 180/120 and you feel unwell, call 911. Otherwise your care team monitors it and will contact you.

How do I know my reading was sent?

iPhone: a box says Reading sent and “Shared with your care team.” If it says Reading saved, it will send by itself when you are back online.

STUB, Android answer: pending.

What if I miss a day?

That's okay — just take your reading the next day. Try to take readings at the same times each day for the best results.
